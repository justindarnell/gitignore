# Calibration report

_Generated by `tools/calibrate/report.mjs` from the raw files in `calibration/results/`._

## Summary

The engine was calibrated with an automated framework (`tools/calibrate/`) that runs the real
engine headlessly in parallel worker threads and scores each candidate against historical
benchmarks computed from English league data (`data/history/derived/benchmarks.json`,
produced by `tools/history/benchmarks.mjs`).

* **Selected model (0.7):** the 0.6 model plus *relative ownership* (finalist 1 of the
  ownership validation on the fresh `holdout4-*` seeds). Holdout score **0.284**
  (short-term 0.203, 50-year 0.320, 100-year 0.411), against
  0.325 (0.211 / 0.438 / 0.441) for the 0.6 model on the same seeds.
* The 0.6 ownership-ambition change was not adopted; 0.7 redesigned it (see "0.7: relative ownership").
* **Uncalibrated starting model** (0.5 search) on the `holdout2-*` seeds: short-term 0.643, 100-year 2.132.
* A score of 1.0 means a metric is on average one tolerance away from its historical target; scores are
  weighted means of squared z-values capped at 3σ (see `tools/calibrate/targets.mjs`).
* About **1864k simulated seasons** in total across all phases
  (1504k recorded in the result files plus ~360k from earlier screening runs that were superseded when the model structure changed).

## Method

1. **Historical benchmarks** (`docs/DATA-SOURCES.md`): 1888/89–2025/26 English league tables rebuilt from
   match results; date ranges are explicit for every target (e.g. Premier League 1995/96–2025/26 vs
   2015/16–2025/26; post-war top flight 1946/47–2025/26). Recent trends and long-run averages are
   kept separate and blended only where the brief asks for both (documented per target).
2. **Two score groups.** *Short-term* metrics are measured on independent first seasons (2026/27 start);
   *long-term* metrics on 50–100-season worlds. The final score weights them equally so a model cannot
   buy long-term diversity with unrealistic seasons, or vice versa.
3. **Screening with common random numbers.** Every configuration in a phase uses the same seeds
   (`screen-*`), which removes most comparison noise.
4. **Search phases.**
   * Short grid: 96 configurations × 150 first seasons (match strength, seasonal variance, per-match noise, base goals, Dixon–Coles ρ).
   * Long search: 500 Latin-hypercube configurations × 6 runs × 60 seasons over 23 parameters.
   * 6 rounds of joint local refinement (short + long score) around the best candidates, shrinking step sizes, 8–16 runs × 80 seasons per candidate.
   * Holdout validation on **unused seeds** (`holdout-*`, then a fresh `holdout2-*` set after the model structure changed): 500 first seasons, 12 × 50-season and 10 × 100-season worlds per finalist.
   * One-at-a-time sensitivity analysis (±25%) of the selected model.
5. **Structural changes driven by evidence, not by single runs.** Several metrics failed in *every*
   sampled configuration, which showed missing mechanisms rather than bad parameter values.
   Each fix was added as a *searchable parameter* and re-validated:
   * *Rank-based economics* (merit/European/parachute money): with division-average revenue only,
     divisions stratified over time, promoted lower-league clubs struggled and the top flight flattened.
   * *Partial persistence of seasonal performance*: the short-term grid alone cannot separate transient
     luck from persistent quality; fully transient luck made every promoted club regress and inflated
     top-four turnover.
   * *Asymmetric recovery, stability-damped development cycles, promotion momentum.*
   * *Consecutive-title fatigue* (renewal gets harder each successive title, no hard cap): removed
     5–6-title streaks that the first validated model still produced (record in history: 4).
   * *Consistent summer adjustment in season one*: clubs that changed division for 2026/27 receive the
     same promotion/relegation squad adjustment the engine applies at every later rollover.

## Post-validation change: soft saturation of expected goals

Inspecting simulated record scorelines showed the far tail was too heavy: 9+-goal league margins
were ~3× and FA Cup 9+ margins ~8× the 1995–2024 rate, because extreme mismatches (Premier League v
non-league) hit the old hard cap of 7 expected goals. Real favourites ease off and underdogs defend
deep, so expected goals above 2.5 now saturate smoothly toward 4.5 (`softCap` in `match.ts`).
Typical matches are untouched. Re-checked on the same `holdout2` seeds (`check-holdout2.json`):
short-term 0.188 (was 0.203), 100-year 0.379 (was 0.411); FA Cup 7+-goal margins now match history (≈59 vs 60 per 10,000 ties).
The tables below show the validated model as selected; the shipped default additionally includes
this saturation (`lambdaSoft` 2.5, `lambdaSpan` 2.0).

## 0.6: ownership ambition

**Why.** Three weaknesses survived every 0.5 configuration: promoted clubs were too uniform (top-half
finishes ≈10% vs 19% in history), lower-league clubs rarely rose to the top flight (≈12% within 25 years
vs 19.6%), and long-run champions were slightly too weak (≈85 vs 87.5 points). All three point to a
missing source of *persistent, club-specific* investment: in reality owners differ enormously.

**Mechanism** (`src/engine/evolution.ts`). Every club has a slow latent trait *ambition* A in [−1, 1]
(−1 asset-stripping, 0 steady, +1 wealthy takeover). Each season A ← ambPersist·A + 0.05·N(0,1); with
probability `ownerEventRate` the club changes hands: a takeover (A = 0.5…1) or a sell-off to
cost-cutting owners (A = −0.45…−0.95). Big clubs and clubs run down by previous owners attract wealthy
buyers; relegated and unstable clubs attract asset-strippers. Ambition then
* shifts the structural target and the squad-upkeep ceiling by `ambTarget`·A rating points,
* scales promotion investment by (1 + `ambPromo`·A),
* skews development shocks toward the upside for ambitious owners (`ambUpside`, mean-preserving), and
* erodes stability when negative. Ownership changes appear in club timelines and the news.

**Seeds.** 25 clubs start from documented ownership situations (e.g. Newcastle's 2021 PIF-led takeover,
Wrexham 2021, Birmingham 2023, Sheffield Wednesday's 2025 crisis); all others start neutral. Each club's
`provenance.ambition` in `clubs.json` records the evidence or "neutral default"; magnitudes are judgements
(`OWNERSHIP` in `tools/ratings/build-db.mjs`).

**Search.** 4 rounds on the screening seeds (`screen-refine-*`, common random numbers),
starting from the 0.5 `DEFAULT_PARAMS`: round 1 a 60-point Latin hypercube over the five ambition
parameters (everything else fixed), then joint local refinement of the ambition parameters and the
interacting long-term parameters (promoBoost, relegHit, revert, eraSD, promoEra, repWeight, rankEconomics, ceiling, ceilingCost) with shrinking steps.
The 0.5 model (ambition switched off, `AMBITION_OFF`) was carried through every round as a reference.

## 0.7: relative ownership

**Why.** The 0.6 mechanism shifted every club's structural target by its ownership, so asset-stripped
*giants* sank as easily as small clubs and the era's giants were relegated far too often. The 0.6 report
suggested making ownership act on squad quality relative to the club's standing, so that established
giants are protected while newly funded clubs can rise.

**Mechanism** (`src/engine/evolution.ts`). The latent trait, its drift and the ownership events are
unchanged. The structural target gains

`own = head · (ambLift · max(A, 0) + ambDrop · min(A, 0))`, with `head = clamp((ambRepRef − reputation) / 35, 0, 1)`.

*Headroom* is how far a club's reputation is below the elite (`ambRepRef`). A club at or above it gets no
target shift from its owners in either direction, so it is shielded from asset-strippers and also
gains nothing structural from a takeover. A smaller club can be pushed far above (or run far below) what
its standing supports. As a funded club succeeds, its reputation rises and the extra push fades:
by then the club's own standing carries it. The upkeep ceiling rises with a positive shift. The 0.6
channels for promotion investment (`ambPromo`) and development upside (`ambUpside`) remain, and the old
target shift (`ambTarget`) is fixed at 0.

**Search.** 4 rounds on the screening seeds (common random numbers), starting from the 0.6
shipped model (`MODEL_06` in `params.ts`, ownership off): round 1 a 120-point Latin hypercube over the seven
ownership parameters (ambPersist, ambLift, ambDrop, ambRepRef, ambPromo, ambUpside, ownerEventRate), then joint local refinement with the
interacting long-term parameters (promoBoost, relegHit, revert, eraSD, promoEra, repWeight, rankEconomics, ceiling, ceilingCost) with shrinking steps. The 0.6 model was carried through every round as a reference.

## 0.7: the 1992/93 start (out-of-sample test and calibration)

**Why.** The 1992/93 start used the parameters calibrated for 2026/27 and was not checked against what
actually happened. Its world was far more open than the Premier League era: about 13 different champions in
its first 33 seasons, against 7 in reality (1992/93–2024/25).

**Real targets** (`tools/history/real1992.mjs` → `data/history/derived/real-1992.json`): 11 aggregate outcomes of
1992/93–2024/25 and two club-level outcomes for each of the 114 clubs of 1992/93 (top-flight seasons, mean tier), measured
by the same code for real and simulated seasons (`tools/calibrate/real92.mjs`). Three windows: **A** 1992–2008 (used by
the search), **B** 2009–2024 (never seen by the search) and the full period.

**Scoring.** Reality is a single draw, so each outcome is scored by the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) of
the real value against the simulated worlds. It is a proper score: an ensemble only improves by being centred on
reality without being too narrow or too wide. Aggregate CRPS values are scaled by the reference model's run-to-run
spread, and the window score is 0.5 × aggregate + 0.5 × club-level, both relative to the reference (fixed curve), so
the reference scores 1.

**Mechanism** (`levelCurveAt` in `src/engine/evolution.ts`). The economic level of each pyramid rank, which drives
clubs' structural targets, can move from the start's fitted curve toward the fitted 2025/26 curve (mapped by quantile
onto the 114 ranks): weight = `levelPathAmount` × t^`levelPathShape`, t = (season − 1992) / 33. The 2026/27 start has
no path; the engine parameters are the 2026 calibration.

**Remaining gaps (reported transparently).**
* The 1992 world is still more open than the Premier League era: more champions, a weaker dominant club, lower champion points
  and a smaller top-four elite. The 2026 calibration targets blend the Premier League era with post-war history, so part of
  this is by design; the rest is the known champion-points weakness.
* Almost every 1992 Conference club reaches the Football League in the simulation (about 18 of 22) against 6 in reality,
  with or without the path. The Conference is the bottom of the playable pyramid: its clubs never drop out, and new clubs
  never come up from below. This metric cannot improve without a playable sixth tier.
* Wimbledon FC (dissolved in 2004) counts as relegated in real history; simulated clubs never dissolve.
* One real history is a small sample: the club-level coverage (about 86% inside the 80% range) suggests the ensembles are,
  if anything, slightly wide.

## 0.7: administration and points deductions

**Frequency target** (`tools/history/administrations.mjs` → `data/history/derived/administrations.json`, from the table of English
clubs on Wikipedia's "Administration (British football)"): 66 administrations in 1992/93–2024/25, 51 of them in tiers 1–5 (by tier: 2 / 13 / 17 / 12 / 7); per season 1.55 overall, 3.50 in the 2000s and 0.73 since 2010.
The engine aims at about one per season (a blend of the long-run and recent rates), with relative risk per club by tier from the historical shares.
The base rate (`adminRate` 0.0112) was set by simulation to that frequency; it was not searched against the calibration targets.

**Check and rule.** Fixed before running: *on by default only if the combined holdout score is not worse than without administration and no group is worse by more than 0.02*. Fresh seeds `holdout5-`:

| Model | Combined | Short | 50-year | 100-year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Without administration | 0.283 | 0.214 | 0.429 | 0.274 |
| With administration (100%) | 0.273 | 0.214 | 0.297 | 0.367 |

**Decision: off by default.** The combined score and the 50-year group improve, but the 100-year group is worse by more than the allowed 0.02: over a century, administrations add turnover among each era's giants and new top-four clubs. The mechanism is available as the *Financial crises (administration)* setting (100% = the historical frequency), and the Dynamic and Chaotic presets switch it on.
The short-term group is unchanged because administrations start from the second season.

## Why this model was chosen

* Lowest combined holdout score of all finalists, with **no group sacrificed**: short-term and both
  long-term horizons are all below 0.45, and the 50- and 100-year scores agree (no drift over a century).
* Screening score and holdout score are close, so the search did not overfit its screening seeds.
* Title dynamics match history: retention, 3+ title runs and unique champions are within tolerance and
  no 5+ title streak occurred in any 100-season holdout world.
* Historic giants are protected statistically, not by rules: an initial giant reaches League One within
  50 seasons in a minority of worlds (the brief's "unusual, but not impossible").

* Several targets for the modern era are judgements (documented in `targets.mjs`): e.g. how much sturdier today's giants are than post-war giants.
* Initial ratings come from results only (no transfer or wage data), and goal data cannot separate midfield from attack/defence.
